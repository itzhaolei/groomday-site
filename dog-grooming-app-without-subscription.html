What the monthly fee is actually buying

It is not nothing, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. A $49–$99 plan typically includes online booking that clients use themselves, two-way texting with automated reminders, card processing, staff scheduling, payroll, marketing campaigns and a booking website. For a salon with three chairs and a receptionist, that bundle is the business.

The mismatch shows up at one chair. A solo groomer answers her own phone, runs her own card reader, and has no staff to schedule. Paying for payroll and multi-location sync every month to get a calendar is the part that stings — and it is a recurring complaint, not a rare one: reading roughly 1,000 reviews across five competing apps, price and subscription complaints ran at 8–12% of all reviews, and about two thirds of those were one or two stars.

The question almost nobody asks before subscribing

Not "which has more features" — you will not use half of them in year one. The question is: what does this cost me if I keep it for three years? Because that is what you are agreeing to. A $49 plan is not $49; it is $1,764, and it will keep being $1,764 as long as the app is part of how you work.

Three years is not an arbitrary number. Groomers who take software seriously tend to keep it — switching means re-entering clients, re-learning a calendar and re-training whatever booking habit clients have formed. The real comparison is between two totals, not two monthly figures.

A note on "free". A free tier that caps the number of clients is a real limit, not a demo. GroomDay's free version holds 8 clients — enough to run a genuine week, not enough to run a year. The $2.99 is what removes the cap. If a product's free tier is unlimited forever, it is usually being paid for by something else, often your client data.

What a one-time price can and cannot cover

A one-time purchase works when the software does not depend on a server the vendor pays for every month. That is the honest boundary:

If you need a client to book herself at 11pm, no one-time app will do it, and a subscription is the correct answer. If what you actually need is to know what today looks like and what you did to that dog last time, the subscription is paying for infrastructure you are not using.

Six questions to ask before you pay monthly

  1. How many clients will I have in 12 months? If the honest answer fits a free tier, buy nothing yet.
  2. Which of these features did I use last week? List them. The list is usually short.
  3. What happens to my client list if I stop paying? Ask for the export before you import anything.
  4. Is the price per user, per van, or per location? The headline price is rarely the one your situation gets.
  5. Does the price need their card processing? Some lower tiers only apply if you route payments through them.
  6. What is the three-year total? Multiply before you decide, not after.

If you are moving off paper rather than off another app, the 7-day setup plan is the cheaper first step, and the printable day sheet costs nothing at all. Both are worth doing before any subscription starts.

Questions groomers ask

Is $2.99 really the whole price, or is there a catch?

It is a single one-time in-app purchase that removes the 8-client limit and unlocks CSV export and the weekly revenue report. There is no subscription, no renewal and no per-client charge. The app itself is free to download.

Can a $2.99 app really do what a $99/month one does?

No, and it does not try. It has no online booking portal, no automated texting and no card processing. It is a day book: the day at a glance, clients and their pets, visit notes, before-and-after photos, and a revenue summary. If your business runs on clients booking themselves online, you need the subscription.

Where is my data kept?

On your phone, in the app's own storage, and in the backup file you export — not on a server of ours. That is why there is no monthly fee: there is no server to pay for. It also means the backup file is the thing to keep safe; the app can write one that includes the photos, so a new phone restores the full history.

Do prices on this page include tax?

No. All figures are the vendors' published prices in US dollars before tax, and App Store prices vary by country. The $2.99 is the US price; your local App Store shows the local one.

Why name the other products at all?

Because the comparison is checkable. Anyone can open the three pricing pages and confirm the numbers; a page that says "cheaper than the alternatives" without naming them cannot be checked at all.

Photo guide

Dog grooming before and after photos, in under a minute per dog

Most groomers take the photos, then never use them — because the light is different every time, the dog is at a different angle, and the finished shot is a blur of a wagging tail. Fix the setup once, and the same five frames take about sixty seconds at the end of every groom.

Per dogAbout 60 seconds
You needPhone, towel, one window
CostNothing

Why these photos bring bookings and the phone camera roll does not

An owner choosing a groomer is not comparing adjectives, they are looking for proof that their dog will come back looking like the dog they pictured. A matched pair — same corner, same height, same distance — does that in half a second. Twenty unrelated photos from different angles do not, however good the grooms were.

There is a second, quieter effect: a consistent set makes a one-chair studio look like a business with a method rather than a hobby with clippers. That is what turns a page visit into a message.

Set the corner up once, then never think about it again

Pick one spot in the salon and treat it as a small studio. The whole point is that the background never changes, so two photos taken six weeks apart still read as a pair.

The five frames

1

Dry-in, side profile

10 seconds — before the bath

Dog standing on the tape mark, side on, head turned slightly towards the window. This is your "before". Take two: one full body, one of the problem area the owner actually mentioned.

2

Wet, if the coat change is dramatic

5 seconds — optional

Only worth it for matted or heavily coated dogs. The wet frame is what makes the finished photo look like a different animal.

3

Finished, same side profile

10 seconds

Same tape mark, same height, same distance. If you only ever take two photos per dog, take frame 1 and this one.

4

Finished, face

15 seconds

The frame that gets shared. Hold a treat just above the lens, take a short burst, keep the one where the eyes are open and the ears are forward. Eyes closed is the single most common reason a good groom photo gets deleted.

5

The walk-away

10 seconds

Three-quarter angle from behind the dog's shoulder, showing the finish down the back and hindquarters. This one carries the "that is a proper groom" impression more than the face does.

Sixty seconds total. Do it before the owner arrives, not while they are standing at the counter.

Phone settings that stop the blur

  • Lock focus and exposure. Press and hold on the dog's coat until the yellow box locks, then slide exposure down a notch — fur reads brighter than the meter expects.
  • Portrait mode off. It blurs exactly the coat edges you are trying to show.
  • HDR on, filters off. Filters change between sessions, so a pair taken weeks apart will not match.
  • Live Photos off. Half the storage, and no accidental frame in the pair.
  • Burst for the face. Ten frames in a second beats one carefully timed shot of a moving head.

Where to keep them so you can find them later

One album per dog, named by date, and delete the failures the same day. Two frames per visit — dry-in and finished — is enough to build a visible history. Anything more becomes a folder nobody opens.

If the photos live next to the appointment instead of in a camera roll, the history is already attached when the owner asks "how much shorter did we go last time?" — which is also the fastest way to answer that question in the chair.

Permission, in one line

Ask once, in writing, at the first visit. A single line on your intake form is enough: "Photos may be used to show my dog's grooming results." A tick box is consent; a verbal maybe is not.

When you post: crop out house numbers, car plates, children and anything with a name on it. If an owner said no once, that answer covers every future groom unless they change it — and it is worth asking again only if they bring it up.

Turn the photo into a rebooking

The photo is the reason to send the message, and the message is what fills next month. Send the finished pair within 24 hours, while the owner still remembers the dog looking like that.

Hi Sarah — Biscuit's before and after from today attached. Coat came up well, and I took a little more off the back than last time. I've pencilled you in for the same slot in six weeks; say the word if you'd rather move it.
Two lines, one photo pair, one specific next date. Text, not email.

If you use a day book, put the next date in before you put the phone down. A reminder that gets written later usually does not get written.

Questions groomers ask

My salon is dark. Can I still take these photos?

Yes, with one change: turn the dog so the brightest wall or window is to the side, and lock exposure on the coat rather than the background. Avoid the phone's flash directly on the dog — it flattens texture and gives red-eye on the face frame. A single daylight lamp on a stand, placed at 45 degrees, is the cheapest fix.

How many photos should I post per dog?

Two: the matched before and after. A third (the face) is fine if it is sharp. Long carousels of the same groom lose viewers before the last frame, and the pair is the part that communicates the result.

Do I need a separate camera?

No. A phone from the last five years, held at shoulder height with focus locked, is enough. A dedicated camera only helps if you also change how you light and frame the dog, which is the part that actually makes the difference.

What if the owner does not want their dog online?

Keep the photos for your own records and send them privately instead. The pair still does its job in the text message; it just never appears in public. Record the preference so you are not asking the same question every visit.

Why these five columns

Most printable planners are built for meetings, so they waste the only two things a grooming day actually needs: how long the dog takes and what happens next to that dog. The columns above come from how a single groomer's day is really shaped:

ColumnWhat it prevents
Time (30 min)Quoting a 2pm slot to a walk-in when the 1:30 doodle is a 3-hour job.
Dog & ownerTwo "Bella"s on the same day, and calling the wrong number at pick-up.
Breed / coatRe-quoting at the door because nobody wrote "matted underside" when booking.
ServiceDoing a full groom when the owner booked a bath and brush.
NeededThe overlap you forgot: two dogs drying at once is fine, two dogs on the table is not.
NotesLosing the one thing the owner cares about ("no clippers on the tail").

How to use it without re-drawing it every week

  1. Print a stack, not a page. Ten copies live in a clipboard by the tub. A sheet that has to be found is a sheet that stops being used.
  2. Book in pencil. Pencil is what makes a paper book faster than a phone at the counter — and it is why paper survives the wet hands.
  3. Write the time needed, not the time promised. The gap between the two is where every overrun comes from. Note it at booking, and the day stops surprising you.
  4. Copy the day's takings to one line at the bottom. Five seconds at close, and you have a revenue record without a spreadsheet.
Where paper breaks. A paper book cannot tell you that this dog was here six weeks ago, what you charged last time, or that the owner always pays by card. That is the only reason we built an app — not to replace the sheet's layout, but to keep the history behind it. If paper is working for you, keep using paper. GroomDay is free to download and holds the same day view on an iPad, with a customer record behind each dog.

If you are comparing grooming apps

Three questions decide it faster than any feature list:

  • Can you see the whole day at once? If the app opens on a list of clients, you will still keep paper next to it.
  • Does it handle overlap? Solo groomers run a bath dog and a table dog at the same time; software built for one-at-a-time salons cannot express that.
  • Does it work with no signal? Grooming rooms are often the worst coverage in the building.

GroomDay answers yes to those three and deliberately does less elsewhere: no staff scheduling, no online booking page, no Android version yet. If you have employees, a salon-platform product will serve you better.
